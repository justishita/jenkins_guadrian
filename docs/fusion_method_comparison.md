## Limits of this comparison

- Only 10 scenarios carry a label, written by the author. A method can win on them without being generally better, and the labels encode the author's view that independent corroboration should beat one stronger source.
- The methods disagree on the verdict in 1 labelled scenario(s); the accuracy gap above rests on that many cases, so the monotonicity and overlap results carry more weight than the accuracy numbers.
- Neither method is calibrated: scores order hypotheses; they are not probabilities.

## Decision

Provisional: **noisy-OR**, labelled uncalibrated. It is the only method that is monotone (corroboration never lowers a score) and it is the only one that can express independent agreement, which is the point of three agents with different evidence domains. Its known weakness is over-rewarding overlapping evidence; the bounded relevance weights and the capped negative evidence limit but do not remove that. Every report records the weighted-mean score alongside, so the choice can be revisited with real labelled incidents.
